# B. Lore bible: the nine Pujos

> **তুমি কোন পুজো?**
> Everyone in Kolkata has a Pujo. It is never quite the same as anyone else's.

This is the universe the quiz opens onto. The quiz is only the door. What people keep, share, argue about and wear on a tote bag is in this document and in the nine character bibles in [`archetypes/`](archetypes/).

---

## 1. The idea

During Pujo, Kolkata becomes nine cities that share the same streets.

The same lane in Ahiritola is three different places. At 5 am it belongs to someone standing quietly in front of a sabeki protima. At 2 pm it is a queue for bhog. At 3 am it is an empty stage lit by a Chandannagar light artist's work, walked by two friends who haven't slept. Nobody is doing Pujo wrong. They are doing different Pujos.

That is the cultural insight the whole system rests on. Pujo is less a single event than a set of rituals, and every Kolkatan has built a personal version from them: which days, which hours, which paras, which plates, which people. The product gives those versions names.

The archetype answers "how do you Pujo", not "what kind of person are you". That is why it can be claimed proudly, argued about with friends, and extended later to the rest of the year without pretending to be psychology.

## 2. The Pujo Clock

The universe has a structure, and it is a clock. Each of the nine Pujos owns the hour when the city is most theirs, and hands it on to the next.

```
 4 am   Shiuli          Mahalaya on the radio, shiuli on the wet grass, a pandal before anyone else
 8 am   Para Kid        Ashtami anjali, the para queue, flowers passed hand to hand
11 am   Pandal Hunter   The sweep begins: Metro card, spreadsheet, the queue beaten by two hours
 2 pm   Pet Pujari      Bhog, then the long lunch, then the reason the route bends towards Park Circus
 4 pm   Art Kid         Golden light on a jute installation, a placard read twice
 6 pm   Pujo Romantic   Dusk, a rickshaw, laal-paar, the long way round
 8 pm   Dhunuchi        Sandhya arati, the dhaak, smoke, the circle opens
10 pm   Addabaaz        Maddox lawn, fifteen friends, the conversation of the year
 1 am   Night Owl       The crowd thins, the lights are the show, the city is a secret
 4 am   (handover)      The Night Owl heads home as the Shiuli's alarm goes
```

Why a clock:

- **It is true.** Kolkata's Pujo genuinely has shifts. Anyone who has done both a 5 am bonedi bari and a 3 am North Kolkata walk knows they are different festivals.
- **It makes the archetypes a system, not a list.** Every archetype has neighbours. The handovers create stories between them (the Night Owl and the Shiuli pass each other on the Bagbazar ghat steps every year, going opposite ways).
- **It is a content engine.** "It's 1 am. The city is the Night Owls' now" is a push notification, an Instagram story, a playlist drop and a route, all at once. Nine hours a day, five days, forty-five natural moments.
- **It is a recommendation primitive.** Your hour is when your recommendations are best. See [`06-recommendation-graph.md`](06-recommendation-graph.md).

The clock is a lore device, not a rule. A Night Owl can go to anjali. The lore says where they feel most at home.

### Why these nine

The starting brief suggested Night Owl, Pandal Hunter, Para Kid, Pujo Romantic, Food Pandalist, Pandal Art Kid, Pujo Socialite and Main Character. What changed and why:

| Brief | Final | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Night Owl | **Night Owl** | Kept. Universally understood, instantly claimable, and it maps to a real Kolkata practice: the all-night walk. |
| Pandal Hunter | **Pandal Hunter** | Kept. The planner-completionist is one of the most recognisable Pujo types. Differentiated from the Night Owl by strategy rather than hours. |
| Para Kid | **Para Kid** | Kept, and written deliberately away from the "para dada" stereotype (see its character bible, "What they reject"). |
| Pujo Romantic | **Pujo Romantic** | Kept, and widened: it includes people who romanticise the city itself, so it never requires a partner. Bengali name মনকেমন (the untranslatable ache of longing), which is exactly the word DESIGN.md allows Bengali for. |
| Food Pandalist | **Pet Pujari** | "Pet pujo" is how Bengal already jokes about eating. The name existed before we did, which is the test an identity should pass. |
| Pandal Art Kid | **Art Kid** | Shortened. Bengali name থিম-পাগল ("theme-crazy") is how Kolkata would say it. |
| Pujo Socialite | **Addabaaz** | "Socialite" is generic, a little classed, and not something anyone calls themselves. Addabaaz is a Bengali word for a real Bengali institution. |
| Main Character | **Dhunuchi** | "Main character" is global internet slang and reads as vain when a friend assigns it. The dhunuchi dancer is the Kolkata-specific version of the same energy, rooted in a real Pujo moment, and aspirational rather than mocking. "Main character energy, Kolkata edition" survives as a reaction line. |
| (not in brief) | **Shiuli** | Without it, the whole dawn, heritage and quiet half of Pujo had no home: Mahalaya at 4 am, bonedi baris, sabeki idols, Agamani songs. Simulation also showed that without a dawn archetype, heritage lovers were misfiled as Para Kids. |

Nine rather than eight or twelve: nine hours on the clock, nine frames in a 3 by 3 grid, roughly 11% each at a neutral prior (Brewer's optimal distinctiveness: big enough to belong to, small enough to feel distinct; see [`01-research.md`](01-research.md) §4.1), and few enough that a person can name all of them after one conversation.

### Considered and rejected

- **The Homecomer (Probashi).** The Bengali working in Bengaluru or London who flies home for Pujo is one of the most emotional Pujo figures there is. But it is a circumstance, not a way of doing Pujo, and a homecomer can be any of the nine. It became a **status badge** instead (below), which is stronger: "Night Owl, home for Pujo" says more than either alone.
- **The Escape Artist.** People who leave Kolkata during Pujo (Digha, Puri, Darjeeling). Real, but MyKolkata cannot serve them during Pujo. They are covered by the Shiuli's crowd-avoidance and by the Far Away badge.
- **Anything built on a deity.** "Which goddess are you?", or archetypes named after Durga's children or their vahanas. The owl is Lokkhi's pyancha, the peacock is Kartik's. It would be clever and it would be a mistake. It turns worship into a personality test, it excludes the non-Hindu Kolkatans for whom Pujo is also home, and it invites exactly the controversy a young brand cannot absorb. A single affectionate wink in the Night Owl's lore is as close as the system goes.
- **Ghoti versus Bangal.** Beloved banter, but it sorts people by family origin and Partition history. Not an input, not a badge.
- **Sixteen types, MBTI style.** More types means thinner communities and less shareable names. Retest instability is also well documented for that design (§5 of the research doc).

## 4. Rules of the universe

These apply to every piece of lore, copy, image and merch, now and later.

1. **Sarbojanin.** The word on half the pandal banners in the city means "for everyone". Pujo in Kolkata is a city festival as much as a religious one, and UNESCO's inscription specifically praised its inclusion across religion, gender and class. Every archetype must be claimable by a Muslim Kolkatan from Park Circus, a Marwari student from Burrabazar, a first-time visitor from Pune, and a grandmother in Shobhabazar.
2. **Ritual is culture, never belief.** Archetypes may take part in anjali, arati, bhog and sindoor khela, and the lore can love those moments. It never asks about faith, never ranks devotion, and never implies that one way of being at Pujo is more authentic than another.
3. **Aspirational first, teasing second.** Every archetype has a light (what makes them proud) and a shadow (the flaw their friends tease them about). The shadow is always affectionate and always something the person would put in their own bio. Never a class marker, a body joke or a gender joke.
4. **Specific over generic.** Name the lane, the dish, the hour, the song. "Mitra Café's fish kabiraji at 4 pm" beats "delicious street food". This is DESIGN.md's voice rule and the single biggest thing that separates this from a translated personality quiz.
5. **Write for someone who already belongs.** Never explain Bengali culture to Bengalis. Let the visitor follow. Mahalaya is mentioned, not defined; the newcomer path in the quiz exists for the rare time a definition is needed.
6. **No deity caricature, no idol faces on cards.** The protima's face never sits under UI or text (DESIGN.md §10). The goddess is present in the lore as the reason for the season, never as a mascot.
7. **Every archetype is gender-neutral.** Lore is written in the second person. Fashion references always include more than one silhouette. Romance never assumes a gender or a couple.
8. **Nobody is a lesser Pujo.** No archetype is the "basic" one. The quiz, the share card and the matching system must never imply a hierarchy.

## 6. Relationships between the nine

Every pair of archetypes has a kind of relationship. The kinds drive the compare feature, squad copy and, much later, Pujo Match. The full copy deck is in [`07-pujo-match.md`](07-pujo-match.md) §6.

- **Kin:** the same Pujo. Easy company.
- **Complement:** different strengths that fit together. The best travel partners.
- **Spark:** opposites. Hard to plan with, great to tell stories about.
- **Crossover:** everything else. The copy is generated from what they share and where they differ.

The three spark pairs worth building content around, because the stories write themselves:

- **Night Owl and Shiuli:** they only ever meet at 4 am, one ending, one beginning. "You'll only ever overlap for twenty minutes a day. Make them count."
- **Pandal Hunter and Addabaaz:** forty pandals against one bench at Maddox.
- **Dhunuchi and Shiuli:** the centre of the circle and the person who came before the circle existed.

## 7. Layers on top of the archetype

The archetype is one word. What makes it feel personal, rather than one of nine boxes, are the layers:

| Layer | Example | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary** | Night Owl | The model's top result. |
| **Streak** (secondary) | "with a Pet Pujari streak" | Second result, shown when it is at least 12% likely. In simulation, about half of two-archetype blends surface both. |
| **Pure** | "Pure Shiuli" | No secondary above 12%. Rare, and treated as a badge. Night Owls and Shiulis get it most often, because theirs are the most single-minded Pujos. |
| **Squad role** | "the Navigator" | Assigned inside a squad, relative to the other members (fourteen roles, one per dimension; see `model/archetypes.json`). |
| **Status badge** | Home for Pujo; First Pujo; Far away | Asked after the reveal. A homecomer Night Owl gets compressed, high-density routes; a far-away Pet Pujari gets recipes and delivery. |
| **Team badges** | Team Sabeki; Team Theme; Last Metro; First Metro | From the rapid-fire round. Pure fun, very shareable, not identity-defining. |
| **Your hour** | 1 am | From the Pujo Clock. |

Together these produce hundreds of distinct combinations from nine names, which is what lets a result feel personal without inventing a hundred archetypes: "Night Owl with a Pet Pujari streak, home for Pujo, Team Sabeki, the Navigator of your squad."

## 8. Language rules

- **Names:** the English name is what people say aloud. The Bengali name is the loud typographic element on every card, set in Noto Sans Bengali at weight 700 (DESIGN.md §4.3: Bengali is the emphatic voice). Both always appear together on identity surfaces.
- **Balance:** English carries the structure, Bengali carries the feeling. Around one Bengali word or phrase per sentence at most in lore, and only words people actually say during Pujo: thakur dekha, anjali, bhog, para, adda, dhaak, bhasan, Bijoya, asche bochor abar hobe.
- **Transliteration:** use the spelling Kolkata uses on its own signs and menus (Pujo, not Puja, in product copy; phuchka, not puchka; Shobhabazar in running text, Sovabazar only in official names).
- **Voice:** second person, present tense, short sentences. Observant, not promotional (DESIGN.md §12). No exclamation marks. No emoji in product copy. Humour lives in specifics ("Your feet will thank you. Your photos won't."), never in mockery.
- **Plural and possessive:** "a Night Owl", "Night Owls", "the Night Owls' hour". "An Addabaaz", "Addabaaz" as a plural too (like "a sheep").

## 9. Writing new lore: checklist

Before anything ships (a new reaction line, a push notification, a merch slogan, an archetype variant for Christmas):

- [ ] Would all nine archetypes' people be happy to read it?
- [ ] Could a non-Bengali, non-Hindu Kolkatan claim it?
- [ ] Is every place, dish, song and custom real, correctly named and correctly placed?
- [ ] Does it name something specific?
- [ ] Is the teasing something the person would put in their own bio?
- [ ] Does it avoid the idol's face, deity jokes, caste, class, body and gender?
- [ ] No exclamation marks, no emoji, sentence case?
- [ ] If it mentions a real business, is that editorial (not paid), and has it been checked that the place still exists?
