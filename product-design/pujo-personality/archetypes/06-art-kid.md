## 2. Lore

You went to Kumartuli in September, before anyone else was paying attention.

The lanes were narrow and damp and full of half-made goddesses: straw bodies on bamboo frames, clay drying in the sun, a woman smoothing a cheek with her thumb. Nobody noticed you, which is how you like it. You took forty photographs of hands.

Most people see a pandal. You see a proposal. Someone decided that this year the city would walk through a room made of ten thousand clay cups, or jute woven like a textile, or a wall that breathes with light. Someone argued for it in a committee meeting in March. Someone welded it. An artist like Bhabatosh Sutar spent months turning a story into a building, and the Chandannagar light artists wired a sky onto it. You read the placard every time. You usually knew already.

Kolkata's open secret is that for a few days every year it becomes perhaps the largest public art exhibition anywhere: made by thousands of hands, free, and gone in a week. UNESCO noticed. The British Council counted what it was worth. You just go and look, properly, for longer than anyone around you.

Your friends have learnt to leave you at the good ones and collect you forty minutes later. You're usually still in the same spot, watching the light on one corner at four in the afternoon. That's your hour: the sun is low enough to come in through the side of the pandal and turn everything gold.

You have opinions about the lighting design. You share them. You carry a camera that shoots film, and you use it on the dhaaki's hands and never on yourself.

And every Dashami, when the protima goes into the river, you feel what artists feel: that the point of a masterpiece might be letting it go.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Materials in close-up: jute weave, terracotta, raw clay, bamboo lashings, shola pith. Hand-lettered placards. Artisans' hands. Installation views with a tiny person for scale. Golden side light. Contact sheets. Film frames with their sprocket holes, which echo MyKolkata's own film-strip band.

**Typography.** The Art Kid's card is the only one set on paper: Obsidian type on Pearl, DESIGN.md's light mode, with Ruby as the accent. It is laid out like a museum wall label: "Art Kid. Mixed media: jute, light, patience. Kolkata, 2026." থিম-পাগল at weight 700 in Obsidian.

**Photography style.** Architectural and material detail. Natural side light. The soft, true colour of good film stock. Careful framing. Scale shots. Square and 4:5.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Soft Pearl `#F2F1ED`. Accent: Ruby Red `#98111E`. Grade: natural daylight, clay greys, jute ochres, soft contrast, true colour.

**Textures.** Clay, straw, jute, terracotta, shola pith, bamboo lashings, canvas, paper placards, film negatives.

**Symbols.** The chalchitra sigil. The placard. The brush. The film frame.

**Bengali cultural references.** Kumartuli and *chokkhu daan*, the painting of the eyes on Mahalaya. The chalchitra behind the protima. *Daaker saaj*, the shola-pith ornaments. Satyajit Ray as designer and typographer (his posters, his *Sandesh* illustrations, the typefaces that carry his name). The Bengal School, Abanindranath Tagore's wash technique (revived in a 2025 pandal), Jamini Roy, Nandalal Bose, Ganesh Pyne. Kalighat pat and the scroll painters of Bengal. The Government College of Art & Craft, where many Pujo artists trained.

**Fashion references.** Handloom from small labels. Block prints. Thrifted jhumkas. One strange ring. A gallery tote. A Kalighat-pat print shirt. Kolhapuris. Paint on the jeans, not on purpose.

**Music references.** Bangla indie. Post-rock (Aswekeepsearching). Satyajit Ray's own film scores. Sarod and sitar. Kolkata's experimental electronic scene.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** theme, concept, material, installation, placard, *shilpi* (artist), *kaaj* (the work), Kumartuli, chalchitra, light design, scale.
- **Phrases:** *Placard ta porechhish?* (Did you read the placard?) *Kaaj ta dekh.* (Look at the work.) *Eta ke baniyechhe?* (Who made this?) *Ek minute daara.* (Wait one minute.)
- **Tone:** curious, earnest, generous with what they know. Nerdy, never snobbish.
- **Humour:** self-aware about being "that friend" at the pandal.
- **Slang:** "concept pandal", "the Sutar one", "Sharad Shamman bait", "main-character lighting".
- **Bengali and English:** 65/35.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** Handloom and small-label pieces, thrift, one statement accessory, a canvas tote, a film camera, sandals they can stand in for a long time.

**What they eat.** Café lunches between pandals (Hindustan Park and Southern Avenue: Sienna, Roastery Coffee House). A phuchka, as cultural research. Chai in a bhaar in Kumartuli. Frequently forgets to eat until a Pet Pujari intervenes.

**Where they go.**

- *Before Pujo:* Kumartuli in September, when the frames are straw and the clay is wet.
- *Theme pandals:* the award shortlists, the artists they follow, and the clusters where concept pujos concentrate: the South arc, Behala, the North line, Salt Lake. The 2025 standouts give a sense of what they look for: Tala Prattoy's centenary theme by Bhabatosh Sutar; Kashi Bose Lane's tribute to Leela Majumdar; Samaj Sebi's *Pather Panchali*; Hindusthan Park's puppetry theme; Santoshpur Lake Pally's revival of Abanindranath's wash technique; Arjunpur Amra Sabai's stainless-steel protima; New Town's biodegradable alpona carpet. The 2026 list is built when the themes are announced.
- *Galleries and museums:* Experimenter (Hindustan Road, Ballygunge Place, and its space at the Alipore Museum), Emami Art at the Kolkata Centre for Creativity, the Academy of Fine Arts, Birla Academy of Art and Culture, the Indian Museum, the Victoria Memorial.
- *Neighbourhoods:* Kumartuli, Ballygunge and Hindustan Park, Behala, Salt Lake.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Placard*: Bangla indie, post-rock, Ray's scores, sarod. Mood: absorbed.

**Who they hang out with.** One friend, or the camera. Adopted by Hunters (who get them to the pandals) and by Romantics (who see beauty in the same places). Their favourite subjects are Dhunuchis.

**Who they'd date.** Someone who will stand still next to them for twenty minutes without asking to leave. Kin: another **Art Kid**, a **Pandal Hunter**. Complement: a **Pujo Romantic**, a **Shiuli** (heritage craft), or a **Dhunuchi** (the muse and the lens). Spark: a **Pet Pujari**, for whom the placard is between them and the phuchka.

**What content they consume.** Pandal art pages. Artists' process videos. Kumartuli photography. Design history (Ray's typography and posters). Gallery programmes. Sustainable-material pandals. Photography zines.

**What they post.** Material close-ups. Placards. The artist's name, tagged. A contact sheet. Film scans, two weeks after Pujo. One installation shot with a tiny person for scale. A carousel: "five details you missed".

**What they say.**

1. "Did you read the placard?"
2. "Who made this? No, really, who?"
3. "Look at the light on that corner. Four o'clock does that."
4. "It's jute. The whole thing. Jute."
5. "Give me ten minutes. Okay, twenty."
6. "The lighting's Chandannagar work. You can tell."
7. "Sabeki craft is art too."
8. "*Ek minute daara.*"

**What makes them proud.** Naming the artist before reading the placard. Photographing a detail nobody else noticed. Calling the award winner before it was announced. Going to Kumartuli before the season started. A finished roll of film.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** Being called pretentious (the Art Kid shares; they don't gatekeep). Being lumped with the Hunter ("I don't count them"). The idea that "art" only means theme pujos: the shola crown and the chalchitra are art too. Being framed as a content creator; they're behind the lens.

**Merchandise.**

- A tote in museum-label layout: "Art Kid. Mixed media: jute, light, patience. Kolkata, 2026."
- A pocket sketchbook embossed with the chalchitra arch.
- Risograph prints of pandal details, commissioned from local artists with a revenue share.
- A film-camera strap.
- Postcards of Kumartuli hands, made only with the artisans' consent and a share of revenue.

**Brand partnerships (future categories).** Paints (the category behind Kolkata's best-known Pujo art award). Art supplies. Cameras and film labs. Galleries, museums and design schools. Sustainable materials. Lighting.

**Community possibilities.**

- **Kumartuli Sundays.** Respectful photowalks: ask first, no flash in faces, buy something from the artisans.
- **Meet the makers.** Talks with pandal artists after Pujo, when they finally have time.
- **The Pandal Archive.** A living, credited archive of every year's themes and artists: a cultural asset, a reason to come back all year, and the best search content the product could own.
- **Five details.** A user-generated carousel format with artist credits.
- **Beyond Pujo:** a gallery-night calendar, the Chandannagar lights in November, the Book Fair's design stalls.
