## 2. Lore

You know the dhaaki's name.

He comes from a village in Nadia, and he has played your para's Pujo for longer than you've been alive. Every year around Panchami somebody from the committee goes down to Sealdah station to find him among the others, and every year he's there, and every year he asks after your mother.

Your Pujo doesn't start on Shashthi. It starts in August at the first committee meeting, where the same three people argue about the same budget. It continues through the evenings when the decorators put up the bamboo, and the afternoon the protima arrives on a truck and the whole para comes out to watch her carried in, slowly, a dozen hands on the frame.

Then the five days, which are not five days but one long day with short naps.

On Ashtami morning you're in the anjali queue in new clothes with a fist of flowers, between an aunty who has known you since you were four and a cousin who flew in from Bengaluru last night. Afterwards you serve bhog: khichuri, labra, beguni and a spoon of chutney on sal-leaf plates, to anyone who walks in. In the evening there's the function: somebody's niece reciting Sukumar Ray, somebody's uncle singing the same Manna Dey song as last year, and it's still good. At night you sit on a red plastic chair with the people you grew up with and watch the whole city pass through.

Your college friends come to see "your" Pujo, and they suddenly understand why you never want to go anywhere else.

On Dashami you cry a little. Everyone does. Then the Bijoya hugs, the sweets, the walk behind the truck to the ghat, and the para's loudest shout of the year: *asche bochor abar hobe*.

It will happen again next year. You'll be there. You always are.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Red and blue plastic chairs in rows. Tarpaulin ceilings. Bamboo scaffolding. Marigold strings. A volunteer badge on a lanyard. The para's hand-painted banner. Three generations in one frame. Steel buckets of khichuri. The pandal seen from inside, looking out at the street.

**Typography.** পাড়াতুতো at weight 700, Pearl on Deep Bordeaux. The Para Kid card has one extra slot: the name of their para, written the way they write it, in Bengali or English (DESIGN.md §4.3 permits Bengali for place and para names as people write them).

**Photography style.** The para uncle's flash photography, done well: candid, warm, crowded. Tungsten and tube light. Hands passing flowers. Aunties laughing. Children with balloons. Wide shots of the para gathered.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Deep Bordeaux `#3F0D12`. Accent: Crimson Silk, once, on the sigil. Grade: warm tungsten, the blue and yellow casts of tarpaulin, full saturated reds from saree borders and marigolds.

**Textures.** Tarpaulin. Marigold. Sal leaf. A steel bucket. Bamboo. Red plastic chairs. The leather of the dhaak.

**Symbols.** The dhaak sigil. The volunteer lanyard. The plastic chair. The shankha.

**Bengali cultural references.** Paratuto relatives. The committee and its receipt book. The *function* and the *jolsha*. Children reciting Sukumar Ray. *Bijoya'r pronam* and *kolakuli*. *Asche bochor abar hobe*. Kojagari Lokkhi Pujo five days later, when the para's aunties are still distributing leftover sweets.

**Fashion references.** New clothes for each day (*pujor jama*). Laal-paar or tant for anjali; a white panjabi. The committee T-shirt for work shifts. Kolhapuris. A gamcha for bhog duty.

**Music references.** The dhaak above everything. The para loudspeaker's playlist: Mahishasuramardini at dawn, old Bengali Pujo songs through the day, Bollywood at the evening function. Rabindrasangeet at the jolsha. *Dola re dola* for the dhunuchi competition.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** *para*, committee, bhog, anjali, function, jolsha, *pronam*, *kolakuli*, Bijoya, *amader para* (our para), and the whole kinship register: *kaku, kakima, mashi, pishi, dada, didi*.
- **Phrases:** *Amader parar pujo-tai best.* (Our para's Pujo is the best.) *Bhog kheye ja.* (Have the bhog before you go.) *Kakima, pronam.* *Asche bochor abar hobe.*
- **Tone:** warm, loud, affectionate, teasing.
- **Humour:** para politics (the same secretary for eleven years), aunties' questions ("what are you doing these days?"), the same song every year.
- **Slang:** "para gang", "committee kid", "bhog duty", "chair duty".
- **Bengali and English:** 50/50. The most Bengali of the nine, because the para speaks Bengali.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** New clothes every day, as tradition demands. Ashtami: laal-paar, tant or a crisp white panjabi. Committee tee and lanyard for shifts. Kolhapuris or sandals. A gamcha over the shoulder on bhog duty.

**What they eat.** Bhog above everything: khichuri, labra, beguni, payesh, chutney. The para stall's chowmein and egg roll. The phuchka stand outside. Bijoya sweets and savouries (nimki, narkel naru, ghugni). Navami's mutton at home for those who eat it; chhanar dalna and luchi for those who don't. Price: mostly free, because it's the para's. Timing: lunch at the pandal.

**Where they go.**

- *Pandals:* their own, above all. One evening for the neighbouring paras, to compare. The sabeki community Pujos that feel like theirs (Bagbazar's old-style protima and fair, Kashi Bose Lane, which has been going since 1937).
- *Neighbourhoods:* home.
- *Events:* the para's function, the dhunuchi competition, the children's drawing competition, the blood donation camp many committees run, the bhasan procession, the Bijoya Sammilani a few weeks later.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Amader Para*: dhaak, old Pujo songs, a Rabindrasangeet, one Bollywood Pujo song for the function. Mood: warm, loud, nostalgic.

**Who they hang out with.** Everyone, across three generations: family, cousins, the aunties on the front row, childhood friends, homecomers back from Bengaluru and Pune, and the college friends they bring along to show off "their" Pujo.

**Who they'd date.** Someone who understands that Pujo means family, and who will say *pronam* to the aunties without being told. Kin: a **Shiuli** (tradition), a **Dhunuchi** (the arati and the dhaak). Complement: a **Pet Pujari** (who treats bhog with appropriate reverence) or an **Addabaaz** (who brings the friends to the para). Spark: a **Pandal Hunter**, who keeps asking to see "just one more para".

**What content they consume.** Their para's own Instagram page (every para has one now). Pujo nostalgia pages. Bhog recipes. Old Pujo songs. The family WhatsApp group's forwards, reluctantly.

**What they post.** The para's pandal from every angle. The bhog plate. The anjali group photo. A video of the dhunuchi competition. A Bijoya selfie with the aunties. "Amader para" with a location tag.

**What they say.**

1. "Our para's Pujo is the best in Kolkata. Don't argue."
2. "Come for the bhog. Stay for the function."
3. "*Kakima, pronam.*"
4. "The dhaaki's here. Now it's Pujo."
5. "I'm on chair duty till ten."
6. "You haven't done Pujo until you've done it from inside a committee."
7. "Why would I go anywhere else? Everyone comes here."
8. "*Asche bochor abar hobe.*"

**What makes them proud.** Serving bhog. Knowing everyone. Their para's pandal getting a mention anywhere. The dhaaki asking after them. Bringing friends to their Pujo. Never having missed Ashtami anjali.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** The "para dada" stereotype: the local strongman, the donation-receipt pressure, the loudspeaker as a weapon. The Para Kid is about service and belonging, never power, and no copy or image may drift towards the other thing. Being called boring or provincial. Any suggestion that the para Pujo belongs only to one community: Kolkata's committees are mixed, and the lore must always leave room for everyone who helps put a pandal up.

## 7. The subculture kit

**Symbols.** The dhaak sigil. The lanyard. The red plastic chair. The para banner.

**Memes and inside jokes.**

- "My para's Pujo is better than your theme pandal."
- "Chair duty is a personality."

- "Every para has one kid who does everything. It's me."

**Social badges.**

- *Bhog duty* — served bhog.
- *Anjali, all three* — Saptami, Ashtami and Navami.
- *Committee kid* — volunteered.
- *Home pandal* — pinned their para on the map.
- *Bijoya rounds* — visited every relative.

**Shareable statements.**

- "Para Kid. Every road leads back to the para."
- "পাড়াতুতো. Ask me which para."
- "8 am is my hour."
- "Our para, since forever."

**Merchandise.**

- **The para tee**, printed with the name of your para, in Bengali, large. The single most likely bestseller in the whole range: people love wearing their para.
- A volunteer-style lanyard with the dhaak sigil.
- A tiny red plastic chair keychain.
- A steel tiffin for bhog leftovers.
- A dhaak enamel pin.

**Brand partnerships (future categories).** Tea and biscuits for committees. Sweet shops for Bijoya boxes. Blood donation drives through NGOs. Children's art supplies for the drawing competitions. Pujo committees themselves, through free, verified para pages.

**Community possibilities.**

- **Para pages.** Committees claim their MyKolkata page for free and keep it accurate: timings, public bhog, events, accessibility, the dhunuchi competition time. The single most useful data partnership the product can have.
- **Para of the Year.** A showcase, not a ranking. Users nominate their para's best moment.
- **Para personality.** "Your para is 40% Para Kids." Shown only when enough people from a para have taken the quiz (see privacy thresholds in [`10-growth.md`](../10-growth.md)).
- **Homecomer meetups** for the friends flying back.
- **Beyond Pujo:** Kojagari Lokkhi Pujo, Kali Puja at the para, Saraswati Pujo at school, the para's winter cricket.
