## 2. Lore

There's a patch of grass at Maddox Square that has been yours, in your head, since you were sixteen.

It isn't yours. Thousands of people sit on it every Ashtami night. But every year, at about ten, your people drift towards it from every direction, and every year it works. The school gang arrives from Gariahat. The college gang arrives from the Metro, late. Somebody's cousin from Pune, somebody's flatmate from Delhi who has never seen Pujo, somebody's new boyfriend who has to be interviewed. Nineteen people, a circle on the grass, a dozen bhaars of cha, and the conversation of the year.

That's your Pujo. The pandal is lovely. You saw it. You'll see it again on the way out. But the pandal is the venue. The adda is the festival.

Adda isn't chatting. You know that, even if you'd struggle to explain it to the flatmate from Delhi. It's the long, looping, argumentative, affectionate conversation that goes from this year's themes to which of Mohun Bagan and East Bengal will break everyone's heart this season, to who broke up with whom in 2019, to what is actually wrong with the city, and back again, without anyone steering. Manna Dey sang that the Coffee House adda is gone. You have spent your whole life proving him half wrong.

You are the glue. You made the group chat. You sent the pin. You counted heads at the Metro exit and noticed who was missing. When someone's phone died at Gariahat, you found them, because you always find them. When the crowd pressed in, you made the circle bigger instead of leaving.

Some time after midnight the adda starts to break. The Night Owls peel off to walk North. The Romantics vanish in pairs. The Para Kids go home to their paras. You stay till the last three, because someone has to see everyone home, and because the last hour of an adda is always the best one.

Five more minutes, you say. It has been three hours. Nobody minds.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Circles of people on grass, seen from above. A dozen clay cups. Hands mid-gesture. Laughter as motion blur. Flash-lit group photos. A long table pushed together. Phones glowing with the group chat. The Maddox lawn at night. Coffee House's high ceiling and slow fans.

**Typography.** আড্ডাবাজ at weight 700. The Addabaaz card has an optional line for the squad's name, written however the group writes it.

**Photography style.** On-camera flash, candid, wide, everyone in motion: the "flash dump" aesthetic Gen Z already uses for its own nights out, done well. 28mm.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Ink `#141819`. Accent: Crimson Silk, once, on the sigil. Grade: mixed street light and flash, punchy contrast, warm skin, saturated reds.

**Textures.** Grass. Clay bhaar. A steel kettle. Phone screens. A woven mat. Coffee House's marble tabletops.

**Symbols.** Two bhaars touching. The circle. The group-chat bubble.

**Bengali cultural references.** Adda as an institution. Manna Dey's *Coffee House-er sei addata aaj ar nei*. The *rowak*, the raised stoop of North Kolkata houses where adda has always happened, and Tenida and his gang on their rowak in Narayan Gangopadhyay's stories. Satyajit Ray's *Aranyer Din Ratri*: four friends, one trip, endless talk. Mohun Bagan and East Bengal.

**Fashion references.** Squad colours on Ashtami. A comfortable panjabi or kurta. A saree with sneakers. The jacket or tote that ends up holding everyone's phones and chargers.

**Music references.** Bangla band singalongs (Fossils, Chandrabindoo, Cactus). Anjan Dutt's *Bela Bose*, sung by nineteen people badly. Manna Dey's *Coffee House*. Antakshari on the grass. Bollywood group songs.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** adda, gang, group chat, *koi?* (where?), *chol* (let's go), *boshi* (let's sit), *ar ektu* (a bit more), cha, *khobor* (news).
- **Phrases:** *Koi tora?* (Where are you lot?) *Maddox-e boshchhi.* (Sitting at Maddox.) *Ar paanch minute.* (Five more minutes.) *Chol, ekta cha hobe.* (Come on, one cha.)
- **Tone:** warm, loud, funny, inclusive.
- **Humour:** loving roasts. Group-chat chaos.
- **Slang:** "the gang", "reunion season", "group chat admin", "headcount", "Maddox o'clock".
- **Bengali and English:** 55/45.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** The group's colour for the night. A comfortable panjabi or kurta, a saree with sneakers. The jacket or tote that becomes everyone's locker.

**What they eat.** Whatever fits on a table for twelve: biryani dinners, rolls passed round, the Gariahat stalls, cha in bhaars, a phuchka round for the whole circle. Bijoya get-togethers with too much food. Price: mid, split twelve ways. Timing: late.

**Where they go.**

- *Base camp:* Maddox Square on Ashtami night.
- *Within walking distance of base camp:* Ballygunge Cultural, Singhi Park, Ekdalia Evergreen, the Deshapriya Park cluster.
- *Daytime adda:* the Coffee House on College Street. A North Kolkata rowak.
- *Neighbourhoods:* Ballygunge, Gariahat, College Street, Park Street later, friends' flats in Salt Lake.
- *Events:* house parties, Pujo concerts in a group, antakshari nights, quiz nights, stand-up, the Bijoya Sammilani.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Maddox O'Clock*: every song the whole circle knows the words to. Mood: warm, loud, together.

**Who they hang out with.** Everyone. They are the bridge between the Night Owls, the Pet Pujaris and the Dhunuchis, and the reason all three end up in the same photo.

**Who they'd date.** Someone the group approves of, which means someone who survives the interview on the grass. Kin: another **Addabaaz**, a **Night Owl**, a **Pet Pujari**, a **Dhunuchi**. Complement: a **Para Kid**, who brings the para as a venue. Spark: a **Pandal Hunter** (forty pandals against one bench) and a **Pujo Romantic** (a table for two against a table for twelve).

**What content they consume.** Bengali stand-up and meme pages. Football banter. Adda-style podcasts. Trivia. Friends' trip vlogs.

**What they post.** Flash group photos. An "attendance" story. The circle from above. A "who's coming?" poll sticker. The reunion carousel. The squad's Pujo card.

**What they say.**

1. "*Koi tora? Maddox-e boshchhi.*"
2. "Five more minutes."
3. "Everyone's coming. Everyone."
4. "Headcount before we move."
5. "The pandal can wait. Sit."
6. "Who's got a charger?"
7. "*Chol, ekta cha hobe.*"
8. "Nobody goes home alone."

**What makes them proud.** The reunion. The one group photo with everyone in it. Nobody lost. The funniest story of the Pujo. Knowing half of Maddox.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** Being called shallow: adda is Kolkata's intellectual and emotional institution, not small talk. Being called a party animal or a drinker: adda doesn't need either. Being made the organiser who does all the work and gets none of the credit. The word "socialite", which is exactly the class-coded label this archetype replaced.

**Community possibilities.**

MyKolkata should never run its own event at Maddox during Pujo. The lawn is already at capacity, and adding a crowd to it is a safety problem, not a feature.
