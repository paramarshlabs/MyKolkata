## 2. Lore

There's a moment at sandhya arati when the lamp is raised, the dhaak doubles its beat, and the smoke from the dhunuchi rolls across the pandal like weather.

Most people step back. You step in.

You've been planning this since July. Not the dance: that arrives on its own, the way it arrived for every uncle and aunty who ever held a clay dhunuchi of smouldering coconut husk above their head and turned slowly, wrists steady, while the dhaaki watched their feet. What you've been planning is everything around it. Five days, five looks. A Dhakai jamdani for Saptami. Garad silk with a red border for Ashtami, because some things are not up for discussion. Something with sneakers for Navami, because Navami is long. Alta drying on your feet as you walk. Jhumkas that make a sound when you turn.

Pujo is the one week a year the whole city dresses up and turns out, and you think it deserves your full effort. It isn't vanity. It's attendance. If the city is going to be this beautiful, someone should match it.

You know everyone is filming. You don't mind. You were made for the part where everyone watches, and you make it look like an invitation rather than a show: by the second round there are six more people in the circle, and one of them is a Para Kid's grandfather who hasn't danced since 1998.

Somewhere, an Art Kid is photographing you and will never tell you. On the front row of plastic chairs, the aunties are judging the dhunuchi competition, informally and correctly, and have already decided you won.

On Dashami you're at the front of the bhasan procession, dancing like the goddess deserves a proper send-off, because she does. There's red on everyone's cheeks, the dhaak is in your chest, and the whole street is shouting *asche bochor abar hobe*.

Main character energy, Kolkata edition. Everyone else just calls it Pujo.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Smoke, backlight and motion. Turning bodies blurred by a slow shutter. Red silk, alta, jhumkas mid-swing. The dhaaki's plume. A ring of phones around the circle. Five outfits laid out on a bed.

**Typography.** ধুনুচি at weight 700 in Pearl on Crimson Depth: the most dramatic card in the set. The Latin name in Clear Sans Display. A static smoke texture can sit behind the type at very low opacity, in keeping with DESIGN.md's rule that ground ornament stays below 5%.

**Photography style.** Backlit smoke, the classic dhunuchi shot. Slow shutter with rear-curtain flash for motion trails. Street-style fashion editorial in North Kolkata. 85mm portraits: this is the one archetype where faces are central, so casting, consent and model releases matter most here.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Crimson Depth `#710014`. Accent: Pearl. Grade: high contrast, smoke-diffused backlight, reds full, skin warm, blacks deep.

**Textures.** Silk, jamdani, coconut-husk smoke, brass, alta, glass bangles, the dhaak's plume.

**Symbols.** The dhunuchi sigil. Smoke. The jhumka. The plume.

**Bengali cultural references.** Dhunuchi naach at sandhya arati, and the dhunuchi competitions paras hold. Garad, jamdani, Baluchari, tant and Murshidabad silk. Dhuti-panjabi with kolka motifs. *Dola Re Dola* in *Devdas*, set at a Durga Puja. The sindoor khela sequence in *Kahaani*. Suchitra Sen's screen glamour. The bhasan procession's dancing.

**Fashion references.** Statement Bengali textiles: Dhakai jamdani, Garad, Baluchari, tant. Dhuti with an embroidered panjabi, a Nehru jacket, statement jewellery, kohl. The modern edit: pre-draped sarees, crop blouses, sarees with sneakers, metallic eyeliner, a big red bindi, alta, jhumkas.

**Music references.** Dhaak remixes. Bollywood Pujo songs (*Dola Re Dola*, *Ami Je Tomar*). Usha Uthup's *Ami Shotti Bolchi* from *Kahaani*. Bengali film dance numbers.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** look, *shaaj* (adornment), *sajgoj* (getting ready), dhunuchi, arati, the circle, reel, *ek round aro* (one more round).
- **Phrases:** *Dhaak ta aro jore.* (Louder, dhaak.) *Ek round aro.* (One more round.) *Aajke kon look?* (Which look today?) *Chol, naachi.* (Come on, let's dance.)
- **Tone:** confident, joyful, magnetic, generous.
- **Humour:** self-aware glamour. "Five looks, zero regrets." "The smoke is my lighting."
- **Slang:** "main character energy", "look three of five", "Pujo lookbook", "serve".
- **Bengali and English:** 60/40.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** Five looks. Saptami: a jamdani. Ashtami: Garad or red-bordered silk, or a dhuti with an embroidered panjabi. Navami: the bold one, a saree with sneakers or a crop blouse. Dashami: red, for the sindoor khela and the bhasan. Alta, jhumkas, a bindi, kohl, a flower in the hair.

**What they eat.** Light, because of the outfit. A quick phuchka. Something cold. Ice cream. Bijoya sweets. "The photo comes first, then the food."

**Where they go.**

- *Pandals:* wherever the arati is strongest and the dhunuchi competition is on: para pujos known for their evening arati, and North Kolkata's sabeki pujos for the real thing. The big-theme pandals for backdrops. Maddox, to be seen.
- *Shoots:* Kumartuli's doorways and North Kolkata's heritage lanes (with permission, and never inside a family's thakur dalan without being asked).
- *Dashami:* the ghat and the bhasan procession.
- *Events:* dhunuchi competitions, Pujo fashion pop-ups, saree-draping workshops, concerts, the bhasan.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Dhunuchi*: dhaak remixes, Bollywood Pujo songs, Bengali film dance numbers. Mood: heat.

**Who they hang out with.** The dance crew. The Addabaaz (for the party), the Para Kid (for the arati), and the Art Kid who is always, quietly, photographing them.

**Who they'd date.** Someone who will either join the circle or film it well. Kin: another **Dhunuchi**, an **Addabaaz**, a **Para Kid**. Complement: an **Art Kid**, the muse and the lens. Spark: a **Shiuli**, who arrived before the circle existed and will be asleep before it ends.

**What content they consume.** Pujo lookbooks. Saree-draping reels. Bengali textile creators and handloom revival stories. Makeup artists. Dance reels. Kolkata's own designers.

**What they post.** A five-looks carousel. A dhunuchi reel with a dhaak-beat transition. The Ashtami get-ready. Sindoor khela on Dashami. The bhasan video. "Look three of five."

**What they say.**

1. "Five days, five looks. Obviously."
2. "When the dhaak starts, I start."
3. "Hand me the dhunuchi."
4. "One more round."
5. "Is the light good here? Okay, now."
6. "Everyone in. Nobody watches from the side tonight."
7. "Garad for Ashtami. That's not a choice, that's the law."
8. "The smoke is my lighting."

**What makes them proud.** Pulling strangers into the circle. The reel everyone reposts. The aunties' approval. Winning the dhunuchi competition, or being robbed of it. The Ashtami look. Keeping heritage textiles alive by wearing them.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** Being called vain, shallow or an attention-seeker; the whole archetype is written to be the generous, magnetic version of "main character". Any mockery of dhunuchi naach, which is devotion and art at once. Body-shaming of any kind. Gendered rules about who dances or who dresses up. Being reduced to "influencer": they are the life of the festival, with or without a following.
