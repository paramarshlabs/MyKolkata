## 2. Lore

You were the child who knew where the phuchka stall was before you knew where the pandal was.

It's not that you don't love the pandals. You do. It's that every pandal in Kolkata is next to something, and you know what. The sabeki protima at Bagbazar comes with a fair full of telebhaja. Shobhabazar is a short walk from Mitra Café, where the fish kabiraji has been fried the same way since 1920. Ahiritola is on the way to Niranjan Agar's egg devil: a whole egg wrapped in mince and crumbs, invented by a cook who taught himself. Your route is a food map with pandals on it. You have never once been lost.

Your Pujo has a structure, like any serious devotion. Morning: luchi and alur dom at home, out of respect. One o'clock: bhog, wherever a para will have you: khichuri and labra off a sal leaf, the best meal of the year, and it's free. Four o'clock: a cabin in the North, cutlets and chops at a marble table. Eight: biryani, with the potato, obviously, and anyone who argues about the potato can find another friend. Eleven: Park Circus. Two in the morning: a roll, eaten walking, because the Night Owls have to eat too and someone has to show them where.

Your friends call you the Snack Minister. The group chat asks you before it asks Google Maps. You hold opinions about which sweet shop makes the real rosogolla, and you have never lost that argument, because you bring samples.

Then Dashami and the Bijoya rounds, which are the Pet Pujari's Olympics. Every relative, every house: a plate of nimki, narkel naru, a sandesh, a glass of water, and one more rosogolla to be polite. You go to all of them. It would be rude not to.

Thakur dekha is the excuse. It has always been the excuse. The goddess, you suspect, understands.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Steel plates, sal leaves and newspaper cones. Paper-wrapped rolls. Top-down tables crowded with plates. Hands tearing luchi. Steam. The phuchka-wala's hands. Glass jars of sweets. The black steel of a tawa. Cabin interiors: marble tabletops, wooden partitions, a handwritten menu.

**Typography.** পেটপুজারি at weight 700. The Pet Pujari card can carry a small "today's menu" block, laid out like a cabin's handwritten board, in Clear Sans Text and Ash.

**Photography style.** Real stalls and real plates, never styled. Top-down on steel, marble and newspaper. Hard flash for street food at night; window light in cabins. Hands always in frame. Macro on texture: the crust of a beguni, the crumb of a devil.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Ink `#141819`. Accent: Taxi Yellow (mustard-oil flame, turmeric, the golden crumb). Grade: warm, glossy, saturated reds and yellows, deep shadows.

**Textures.** Sal leaf, newspaper, steel, marble, clay bhaar, glass sweet jars, a sweet box tied with string.

**Symbols.** The sal-leaf plate. The roll in paper. The clay pot. The steel tumbler.

**Bengali cultural references.** *Pet pujo* and *bhojonrosik* (the classic Bengali word for a lover of good food). The cabin culture of North and central Kolkata. The Bijoya platter. The biryani's potato and the story that it came with Wajid Ali Shah's exile to Metiabruz. The rosogolla argument, which the Pet Pujari has personally settled.

**Fashion references.** Loose cotton, forgiving waistbands (worn as strategy, not as a joke about anyone's body), a saree with a pallu that can be tucked away from the gravy, sneakers, and a cloth tote that fits four sweet boxes.

**Music references.** Whatever the stall is playing, loud. One old Bengali song in the cabin, on a radio that's been there longer than the menu.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** bhog, khichuri, labra, beguni, roll, biryani, *aloo*, kabiraji, devil, chop, cutlet, phuchka, churmur, jhalmuri, ghugni, nimki, naru, *mishti*, *khide* (hunger).
- **Phrases:** *Khide peyechhe.* (I'm hungry.) *Aage bhog, tarpor baki shob.* (Bhog first, then everything else.) *Ekta roll hobe?* (One roll?) *Biryani-te aloo chhara hoy na.* (There's no biryani without potato.)
- **Tone:** enthusiastic, generous, authoritative, funny.
- **Humour:** food-first logic applied to everything, including the route and other people's life choices.
- **Slang:** "Snack Minister", "food map", "side dish" (a small pandal next to a great stall), "carb-loading for Navami".
- **Bengali and English:** 50/50. Food is spoken in Bengali.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** Comfortable, loose, stain-tolerant. A kurta, a cotton saree, sneakers, the tote.

**What they eat.** Everything, in order.

| Hour | Plate | Where, for example |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Luchi and alur dom | Home, or a North Kolkata sweet shop's morning kochuri |
| 1 pm | Bhog | Any para that serves visitors (the Bhog Map says which, and when) |
| 4 pm | Cutlets, chops, kabiraji, egg devil | Mitra Café (Shobhabazar), Niranjan Agar (Girish Park), Allen's Kitchen (Shobhabazar), Anadi Cabin's Mughlai paratha (Esplanade) |
| 8 pm | Biryani, with aloo | The biryani houses of Chitpur Road and Park Circus |
| 11 pm | Kebabs and rezala | Park Circus |
| 2 am | Egg-chicken roll | The stall still frying |
| One morning | Chinese breakfast | Tiretta Bazaar at dawn, with the Shiulis |
| One night | Chilli chicken and hakka noodles | Tangra |
| Dashami | Nimki, narkel naru, ghugni, sandesh, rosogolla | Every relative's house |

A vegetarian Pet Pujari route is just as serious: luchi and chhanar dalna, mochar chop, dhokar dalna, shingara, phuchka, jhalmuri, and the whole sweet shop. Diet is a filter the person sets, never a judgement.

**Where they go.**

- *Pandals, chosen by what's next to them:* Bagbazar (for its fair), Shobhabazar and Ahiritola (for the cabins), College Square (for Paramount's sherbet), the Park Circus side of the city, Gariahat and Ekdalia (for the stalls).
- *Neighbourhoods:* Chitpur, Park Circus, Esplanade, Shyambazar, Gariahat, Tangra.
- *Events:* food walks, bhog listings, Pujo food festivals.
- *Year-round:* winter and nolen gur season. Christmas cake from New Market's old bakeries. The evening food walks on Zakaria Street in Ramadan (Kolkata's other great food festival, and a reminder that this city's feasts belong to everyone).

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Pet Pujo*, which is mostly the sound of a tawa.

**Who they hang out with.** A food crew. Every group adopts them as its guide. Night Owls need them for the late menu; Hunters need them to remember to eat; Addabaaz need them to book the table for twelve.

**Who they'd date.** Someone who shares their plate without being asked. Kin: another **Pet Pujari**, a **Night Owl**, an **Addabaaz**. Complement: a **Pandal Hunter** ("you plan the pandals, I'll plan the stops") or a **Para Kid** (who respects bhog properly). Spark: an **Art Kid**, who wants to read the placard while the phuchka goes cold.

**What content they consume.** Kolkata food reels and "legacy eatery" series. Bengali home cooking channels. Food history: the biryani's potato, the rosogolla's lineage. Restaurant openings. Sweet shop debates.

**What they post.** Top-down plates. A food map story with pins. The cross-section of a roll. The bhog plate. The Bijoya sweet-box haul. "Pujo, day three: eleven meals."

**What they say.**

1. "Bhog first. Then everything else."
2. "Biryani without aloo is just rice."
3. "This pandal is fine. The stall outside is incredible."
4. "One roll. Each."
5. "Trust me, Mitra Café is on the way."
6. "It would be rude not to."
7. "*Khide peyechhe.*" (It has been forty minutes.)
8. "The best meal of the year is free and served on a leaf."

**What makes them proud.** Never a bad meal. Being the person the group depends on. Finding a cabin nobody else knew. The Bijoya haul. Knowing the story behind every plate.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** Any body joke, ever: the Pet Pujari is about appetite for the city, not about size, and fat jokes are banned from the whole system. Being reduced to "just a foodie" (they're a historian of the city's kitchens). Vegetarian and Jain Pet Pujaris being treated as lesser. Anything that mocks bhog, which is sacred food and is treated that way.
