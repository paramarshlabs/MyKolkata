## 2. Lore

Your Pujo starts in August.

That's when the first themes leak: a committee posts a teaser, an artist's name turns up in the paper, a replica rumour goes round the group chat. You start a list. By Mahalaya the list is a spreadsheet, with tabs for North, South, Salt Lake and Behala, a column for the nearest Metro station, and a colour code nobody else understands.

The city makes this hard on purpose. Kolkata has around three thousand community Pujos, and the good ones are scattered across forty kilometres of traffic, barricades and one-way diversions that change every evening. Ordinary people pick a side of the city and give up on the rest. You don't give up.

You go early. This year nothing opens before Mahalaya, so the sweep begins the day after: the famous replica at 11 am on Chaturthi, before the barricades, before the queue, before anyone has posted it. You go North by Blue Line and walk between the stations, because once the city fills up, feet are faster than anything with wheels. Three committee secretaries know you by face. They know you by your sneakers.

Your friends call it an obsession. You call it respect. Somebody spent six months building that pandal out of bamboo and clay cups and light. The least anyone can do is see it.

By Navami you've seen more pandals than most people see in ten years of Pujos, and you remember them: the ones everyone saw, and the tiny one in a Behala lane with a theme about a disappearing trade that nobody else will ever know existed.

Feluda had his *magajastra*, his brain-weapon. You have a Metro card, a charged phone and a spreadsheet. The case is the same every year: find everything. You always solve it.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Maps, pins and routes, with the route itself drawn as an alpona line. Metro diagrams. Contact-sheet grids of pandal façades. Sneakers mid-stride. The full front of a pandal in late-morning light, empty.

**Typography.** প্যান্ডেল শিকারি at weight 700. The Latin name in Clear Sans Display. The Hunter's card is the only one that carries a large numeral (their own count, if they choose to add it), always tabular. The count is static on the card; nothing ticks up.

**Photography style.** Typological. Frontal, centred, symmetrical, the pandal as architecture, photographed in the clean light of late morning. Shown as grids, the way the Bechers photographed water towers. Plus motion detail: sneakers, a hand tapping a Metro card.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Slate `#1C2225`. Accent: Taxi Yellow, for pins and the count. Grade: crisp daylight, neutral to slightly cool, high clarity, true colour.

**Textures.** Folded map paper. A Metro smart card. Sneaker mesh. Police barricade tape. A laminated press pass that a committee handed over once and never asked back for.

**Symbols.** The compass-rose sigil. The map pin. The Metro line. A bead-run tally (the alpona dot sequence, read as a count).

**Bengali cultural references.** Feluda and his *magajastra*. *Joy Baba Felunath*, a detective story set during Durga Puja. The Kolkata Traffic Police's annual Puja guide map. The Sharad Shamman shortlists. The city's own reporting of "top pujos" every year, which the Hunter reads in order to disagree with it.

**Fashion references.** Running sneakers, a breathable tee or cotton kurta, a cap, a crossbody with a power bank and a steel bottle. For Ashtami, a cotton saree tucked for walking, or a panjabi with sneakers. Sunglasses for the Chaturthi sweep.

**Music references.** Walking-tempo playlists. Bangla band for motivation (Cactus, Fossils). Bollywood Pujo songs with a fast beat. City-history podcasts between clusters.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** list, route, pin, cluster, *gali* (lane), barricade, diversion, "done", "next", Sharad Shamman, Chaturthi.
- **Phrases:** *Eta hoye gelo.* (That's done.) *Porer ta?* (Next one?) *Metro-te chol, taratari hobe.* (Take the Metro, it's faster.) *Chaturthi-tei dekhe nebo.* (I'll see it on Chaturthi itself.)
- **Tone:** brisk, precise, helpful, secretly proud.
- **Humour:** self-aware about the obsession. "I have a problem. The problem is forty-one pandals."
- **Slang:** "cleared North", "speedrun", "side quest" (a hidden pandal), "boss level" (the viral replica on Saptami night).
- **Bengali and English:** 60/40. Logistics in English, triumph in Bengali.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** Comfort engineered to look intentional. The best sneakers they own. Cotton that breathes. A cap. A small backpack or crossbody. The Ashtami outfit exists, and it is walkable.

**What they eat.** Whatever is between two pandals: jhalmuri in a paper cone, a cutlet eaten standing, bananas and biscuits from the bag, a water refill. One proper meal a day, usually skipped. Price: low. Timing: whenever the route allows.

**Where they go.**

- *Before Pujo (after Mahalaya):* the big-crowd pandals, early. Sreebhumi in Lake Town at 11 am on Chaturthi. Santosh Mitra Square, Mohammad Ali Park and Chaltabagan before the barricades go up.
- *Clusters:* the North line (Tala Prattoy, Hatibagan, Kumartuli Park, Bagbazar) walked between Blue Line stations. The Dum Dum Park cluster. The Salt Lake blocks. The South arc (Deshapriya Park, Tridhara, Hindustan Park, Ekdalia Evergreen, Singhi Park, Maddox Square, Ballygunge Cultural). Behala along the Purple Line.
- *Always:* the Sharad Shamman shortlist the day it's announced, and one hidden side-quest per night.
- *Year-round:* heritage walks, the Christmas lights on Park Street, a lesser-known museum every month, the Book Fair map in one afternoon.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Porer Ta* (Next One): 120 bpm, Bangla band, Bollywood with a beat. Mood: momentum.

**Who they hang out with.** A small team that can keep up. The ideal crew is one Art Kid (who slows them down for the right reasons) and one Pet Pujari (who makes them eat).

**Who they'd date.** Someone who treats a well-planned day as a love language. Kin: another Hunter, an Art Kid. Complement: a **Pet Pujari** (the Hunter plans the pandals, the Pet Pujari plans the stops) or a **Night Owl** (who extends the map into the night). Spark: an **Addabaaz**, who wants one bench and four hours; a **Para Kid**, who won't leave the para.

**What content they consume.** Theme-reveal pages. Award shortlists. Traffic advisories. Metro updates. Pandal photography. "Top ten pandals" reels, which they watch in order to correct them.

**What they post.** Grid collages: twelve pandals, one story. A route map with pins. "Day 2: 38." The end-of-Pujo ranked list (they are the ones who post rankings). The hidden gem, with a location pin, to be useful.

**What they say.**

1. "If we leave at ten we beat the queue."
2. "Done. Next."
3. "It's faster to walk from Girish Park."
4. "This one's on the Sharad Shamman shortlist. Trust me."
5. "Metro, not cab. Never a cab during Pujo."
6. "I saw it on Chaturthi. It was better without people."
7. "There's a small one two lanes away. Side quest."
8. "Forty-one. Don't ask."

**What makes them proud.** Clearing a whole zone. The hidden gem nobody else found. Seeing the viral one before it went viral. Being the person friends call before they leave home. Never, ever getting lost.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** Being told they don't enjoy anything (the hunt *is* the enjoyment). Being called a box-ticker (it's curation at speed). Being lumped in with the Art Kid ("I don't need to read the placard to love it"). And any product mechanic that turns their Pujo into a public race: a leaderboard for most pandals would reward rushing through crowds, which is dangerous at peak hours and not what the Hunter is about. The count stays personal.

**Merchandise.**

- *The Hunter's Map*: a fold-out paper map of the season's clusters, printed once the themes are announced. A real product, and the most useful thing in the merch range.
- A tin of blister plasters with the compass sigil. Every Kolkatan knows the new-shoes blister (*notun juto'r foska*); this is the most Pujo object in the whole collection.
- Socks with the compass. A Metro-card holder. A tally notebook.

**Brand partnerships (future categories).** Comfort footwear (Kolkata has its own footwear institutions). Power banks. Mobile data. Water bottles. Transit information. First aid. Biscuits and energy snacks. Pujo committees themselves, through verified pandal pages.

**Community possibilities.**

- **The Chaturthi Sweep**: a hosted daytime group route before the crowds.
- **Hidden gems**: people submit small pandals worth seeing, editors verify them, and the best become the "side quest" of the night.
- **The Hunter's Map** as a collaborative map that improves every year.
- **Beyond Pujo**: heritage walks, Park Street lights at Christmas, a hidden-museum series, the Book Fair in one afternoon.
