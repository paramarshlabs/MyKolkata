## 2. Lore

Your Pujo begins in the dark, with a voice.

At four in the morning on Mahalaya the radio crackles and Birendra Krishna Bhadra begins the *Mahishasuramardini*, as he has on this morning since the 1930s. Once, in 1976, All India Radio tried to replace it with a new version starring Uttam Kumar, the greatest star Bengali cinema ever had. The city refused. There were protests, the old recording came back, and it has never left. You understand those people completely.

Some people wake up for Pujo. You wake up early for it.

While the city sleeps off the night, you're already out. The shiuli has fallen overnight: small white flowers with orange stems, scattered on wet grass as if someone spilt the season. You pick one up. You always do. Tagore wrote of tying bundles of kaash and stringing garlands of shefali for the goddess, and on these mornings you believe every word.

Your Pujo is North, and old, and quiet. The bonedi baris of Shobhabazar and Jorasanko, where families have held their Pujo in the same thakur dalan for a century or two. The ek-chala protima, the whole divine family in a single frame beneath a chalchitra painted the old way. The shola crown. A priest's voice with nobody else in the courtyard. You'd rather see one of these at six in the morning than forty pandals at night.

On the way home you pass a Night Owl on the Bagbazar ghat steps, finally going to bed. You nod. You are the only two people in Kolkata who have seen that pandal empty.

You know what the grandmothers know: that the Agamani songs welcoming Uma home and the Bijoya songs letting her go are the oldest Pujo music there is. So Dashami doesn't feel like an ending to you. It feels like a daughter going back to Kailash.

And the shiuli will fall again tomorrow, whether anyone is up to see it or not. You'll be up.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Pre-dawn blue. Mist. Shiuli on wet grass. A radio on a windowsill. Courtyards of lime-plastered columns. The ek-chala protima at a distance (never the face under text). Chalchitra detail. Shola filigree. Ghats at sunrise. Steam from a cup of cha. Pujabarshiki pages.

**Typography.** শিউলি at weight 700 in Obsidian on Soft Blush: the only archetype card in light mode on Blush, the colour of first light. Ruby is the accent. The shiuli's orange stem in the sigil uses the light-mode Taxi Yellow (`#C98A1F`), which DESIGN.md permits where the object itself carries that colour.

**Photography style.** Pre-dawn available light. Low contrast, lifted shadows, cool blue with one warm source (a lamp, a tea stall). Wide, quiet, symmetrical courtyards. Macro of the shiuli. Stillness. 35mm and 50mm.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Soft Blush `#FBE4E3`. Accent: Ruby Red `#98111E`, plus the stem. Grade: low contrast, cool dawn blue, one stripe of warm light.

**Textures.** Wet grass. Whitewashed walls. Lime-plaster columns. Old wood. Bakelite. Cotton tant. A clay lamp. Paper.

**Symbols.** The shiuli sigil. The radio. The thakur dalan's column. The shola crown. The shankha at dawn.

**Bengali cultural references.** The *Mahishasuramardini* and Birendra Krishna Bhadra's voice, including *Bajlo tomar alor benu*. The 1976 episode, when the city said no to replacing it. Agamani and Bijoya songs. Uma's homecoming. The bonedi baris: Shobhabazar Rajbari (its Pujo dates from 1757, and it is credited with the *daaker saaj* ornaments), the Daw family of Jorasanko (since 1840), Chhatu Babu–Latu Babu. The Pujabarshiki. Rituparno Ghosh's *Utsab*, a family gathered in an old house for Pujo. Tagore's autumn songs.

**Fashion references.** White with a red border. Soft tant. A grandmother's saree. A crisp white dhuti-panjabi. A jhola bag. Chappals. A shiuli tucked behind an ear.

**Music references.** *Mahishasuramardini*. Agamani songs. Rabindrasangeet for autumn (*Aji sharata tapane*). Nazrulgeeti. Morning ragas. Sarod at sunrise. Later in the year: the Dover Lane Music Conference.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** *bhor* (dawn), shiuli, Mahalaya, Agamani, Bijoya, thakur dalan, ek-chala, *sabeki*, *bonedi*, *shanti* (peace), *chup* (quiet), *pronam*.
- **Phrases:** *Bhor-e uthbo.* (I'll be up at dawn.) *Mahalaya shunechhis?* (Did you hear Mahalaya?) *Chol, bhir hobar aage.* (Come on, before the crowd.) *Uma ghore phirchhe.* (Uma's coming home.)
- **Tone:** quiet, precise, warm, a little wry about being called old.
- **Humour:** about waking everyone up. "I'm not old. I'm early."
- **Slang:** "dawn patrol", "Team Sabeki", "OG Pujo", "bonedi crawl".
- **Bengali and English:** 45/55. The most Bengali in feeling, the least in volume.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** Laal-paar white. Tant. A grandmother's saree. A white dhuti-panjabi. A jhola. Chappals. A shiuli behind an ear.

**What they eat.** The dawn menu. Kochuri and jalebi from a North Kolkata sweet shop as its shutters go up. A Chinese breakfast at Tiretta Bazaar at first light (dumplings and soup: the city's most unexpected Pujo morning). Luchi and alur dom at home. Bhog at a bonedi bari when it is offered. Sandesh from the old sweet houses of North Kolkata. Price: modest. Timing: before nine.

**Where they go.**

- *Bonedi baris:* Shobhabazar Rajbari, the Daw Bari in Jorasanko, Chhatu Babu–Latu Babu, and the other old family pujos of North Kolkata. Visiting hours are the family's to set, photography rules are theirs too, and the product says so.
- *Sabeki pujos:* Bagbazar at dawn.
- *Places:* Kumartuli at sunrise. The Bagbazar and Kumartuli ghats. Belur Math across the river. College Street's Pujabarshiki stalls before Pujo.
- *Neighbourhoods:* Shobhabazar, Jorasanko, Bagbazar, Kumartuli, Chitpur, Belur.
- *Events:* heritage parikramas of the bonedi baris, morning concerts, Mahalaya itself.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Bhor* (Dawn): Agamani songs, autumn Rabindrasangeet, morning ragas. Mood: stillness.

**Who they hang out with.** One or two people. A grandparent. A Para Kid at anjali. A Romantic at the ghat. An Art Kid who appreciates the shola crown as much as they do.

**Who they'd date.** Someone who will wake up early with them without complaining, or at least without complaining much. Kin: another **Shiuli**, a **Para Kid**, a **Pujo Romantic**. Complement: an **Art Kid**. Spark: a **Night Owl** (twenty minutes a day, at dawn) and a **Dhunuchi** (the centre of the circle, against the person who was there before the circle existed).

**What content they consume.** Old Calcutta photo archives. History podcasts. Bengali literature. Classical music. The Pujabarshiki, still, every year. Documentaries about Kumartuli and the bonedi baris. Rituparno Ghosh's films.

**What they post.** Rarely. A dawn photograph. A shiuli in the palm. The thakur dalan's columns. The radio at 4 am on Mahalaya. Long captions.

**What they say.**

1. "Mahalaya at four. I'll wake you."
2. "Before the crowd, or not at all."
3. "It isn't Pujo until I hear that voice."
4. "Sabeki, always."
5. "The shiuli's fallen. Come and see."
6. "*Uma ghore phirchhe.*"
7. "Shobhabazar has done it this way since 1757."
8. "I'm not old. I'm early."

**What makes them proud.** Never missing Mahalaya. The bonedi bari circuit. An empty pandal at 6 am. Knowing the history. Keeping a grandparent's tradition alive, and passing it to a younger cousin.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** "Boring", "old", "aunty-uncle vibes". Any implication of religious gatekeeping: the Shiuli never judges anyone's Pujo as less devout. Being mistaken for a Para Kid ("I don't need a crowd"). Being called an anti-theme snob: they love art, they just love the old more.
