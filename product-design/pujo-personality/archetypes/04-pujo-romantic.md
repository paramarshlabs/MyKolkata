## 2. Lore

You have a Pujo song.

Not a playlist. One song, and it changes every few years, and each one is a different autumn. Right now it might be Anupam Roy. It used to be something your mother hummed from *Saptapadi*: *ei path jodi na shesh hoy*, if only this road would never end. You understood it at eleven. You understand it better now.

For you, Pujo has always been a love story. Sometimes with a person. Sometimes with the city itself, the way it looks at dusk on Ashtami, red borders everywhere, the whole of Kolkata dressed as if it's expecting someone.

You know the rules nobody writes down. That anjali is the best place to stand next to someone without it meaning anything, officially. That a plate of phuchka shared between two people is a complete sentence. That the long way home through the North Kolkata lanes, in a rickshaw, is always worth it. That College Square at night, when the lights lie on the water, is the most romantic place in the city, and you will argue with anyone who says otherwise.

You've watched love stories begin at a pandal gate. You've watched them end on Dashami, at the ghat, in silence, which is its own kind of beautiful. The phuchka-kaku outside the big pandal has seen a thousand of them. You like to think he remembers yours.

Even in the years you went alone, it was a love story. The kaash was out along the Rajarhat roadside, the sky was that particular autumn blue, and from somebody's balcony came Tagore's song about tying bundles of kaash for the goddess: *amra bedhechhi kasher guchchho*. You stood still for a minute. That counts.

Some people have a Pujo. You have a Pujo and a feeling about it, and the feeling has a name, and the name doesn't translate: মনকেমন.

## 4. Aesthetic

**Visual language.** Blue hour against warm pandal light. Two hands. Red borders. Alta on bare feet. A rickshaw hood framing a lane. Rain on glass. Kaash against a grey-blue sky. Reflections on water. A shared plate.

**Typography.** মনকেমন at weight 700 in Pearl. The tagline uses the house caption device from DESIGN.md §9.6: a first line in Pearl, a second line indented 32px in Soft Blush. It is the only archetype whose card uses Blush, the palette's "rare highlight".

**Photography style.** 50mm, shallow depth of field, film grain. Blue hour and warm practicals. Couples and pairs photographed from a distance, candid, often from behind. Details: hands, feet, a hair flower, a shared plate.

**Colour direction.** Card ground: Bordeaux Deep `#260A0E`. Accent: Soft Blush `#FBE4E3`. Grade: soft contrast, warm skin, reds held full, cool blue dusk in the shadows.

**Textures.** Cotton tant. Rain on glass. A rickshaw's leather hood. Kaash plumes. A paper cone. A handwritten note.

**Symbols.** Two kaash plumes. A rickshaw bell. The full moon of Kojagari Lokkhi Pujo, five days after Dashami.

**Bengali cultural references.** মনকেমন itself. *Saptapadi* and its road song. Uttam Kumar and Suchitra Sen. Tagore's autumn songs, including the one that binds kaash and shefali (shiuli) in the same line. Jibanananda's "eyes like birds' nests" in *Banalata Sen*. *Bishorjon* (2017), a love story at the immersion ghat. *Bikele dekha hobe*: "see you in the evening", the oldest Pujo promise.

**Fashion references.** Coordinated but deniable: one person in laal-paar, the other with one red detail. Alta. Jhumkas. A bindi. A flower in the hair, rajanigandha or beli. A crisp dhuti-panjabi. A film camera.

**Music references.** Anupam Roy (*Amake Amar Moto Thakte Dao*). Arijit Singh's Bangla songs (*Bojhena Shey Bojhena*, *Tomake Chai*). Hemanta Mukherjee and Sandhya Mukherjee (*Ei Path Jodi Na Shesh Hoy*). R.D. Burman's *Mone Pore Ruby Roy*. Tagore's autumn songs.

## 5. Language

- **Vocabulary:** *prem*, *bhalobasha*, মনকেমন, *chokhe chokhe* (eye to eye), *shesh bikel* (late afternoon), *golpo* (story), *chithi* (letter), *Sharat* (autumn).
- **Phrases:** *Bikele dekha hobe?* (See you in the evening?) *Chol, ektu hete ashi.* (Come, let's walk a little.) *Ei path jodi na shesh hoy.*
- **Tone:** soft, wistful, knowingly cinematic, with enough self-awareness to be funny about it.
- **Humour:** at their own drama. "I'm not crying. It's the dhuno smoke."
- **Slang:** "Pujo-y prem" (Pujo romance), "Ashtami date", "the long way home", "situationship season".
- **Bengali and English:** 55/45. Bengali lyrics as captions, always.

## 6. The cult

**What they wear.** Laal-paar, tant, alta, jhumkas, a bindi, a flower in the hair, a white panjabi with a red-bordered dhuti, a film camera around the neck. Coordinated with someone, but never matching.

**What they eat.** One plate of phuchka, shared. Daab sherbet at Paramount on College Square. Mishti doi in a clay pot. Ice cream from a pandal stall. A sandesh from a heritage sweet shop. For the "proper" date, a table at Flurys on Park Street. Price: mid. Timing: dusk and dessert.

**Where they go.**

- *The dusk route, North:* the Kumartuli lanes, Bagbazar ghat at sunset, a rickshaw through the lanes, College Square's lights on the water.
- *The dusk route, South:* Maddox Square early in the evening before the crowd, a café porch in Hindustan Park, Prinsep Ghat after dark.
- *Before Pujo:* the kaash fields along the Rajarhat roads.
- *Dashami:* the ghat, quietly.
- *Neighbourhoods:* North Kolkata's lanes, College Street, Ballygunge and Hindustan Park, the river.
- *Year-round:* Kojagari's full moon; Saraswati Pujo in February, which Kolkata already calls its own Valentine's Day; winter rooftop evenings.

**What they listen to.** A playlist called *Ei Path* (This Road): Bengali love songs across seventy years. Mood: warm, wistful, cinematic.

**Who they hang out with.** In twos. Plus one best friend who hears the whole story on Bijoya.

**Who they'd date.** Someone who notices things. Kin: another **Romantic**, a **Shiuli** (dawn at the ghat, together). Complement: an **Art Kid** (who sees beauty in the same places) or a **Night Owl** (who romanticises the city at 3 am). Spark: an **Addabaaz**, who wants a table for twelve when they wanted a table for two.

**What content they consume.** Classic Bengali cinema. Rituparno Ghosh. Poetry pages (Tagore, Jibanananda). Film photography. Couple photographers. Bengali lettering and typography accounts.

**What they post.** Two hands, one plate. A blurred rickshaw ride. The moon. Bengali lyrics as captions. A close-up of alta. The song of the season. On Dashami, a quiet ghat photo and nothing else.

**What they say.**

1. "*Bikele dekha hobe?*"
2. "Let's take the long way."
3. "One plate. Two people."
4. "College Square at night is the most romantic place in Kolkata. I will not be taking questions."
5. "It's not a date. It's anjali."
6. "*Ei path jodi na shesh hoy.*"
7. "I'm not crying. It's the dhuno."
8. "Every Pujo has a song. This year's is this one."

**What makes them proud.** The photo of two hands. The song of the season. Remembering every Pujo by who it was with. Making their friends' love stories happen.

**What would make them reject the archetype.** Being called desperate or dating-obsessed. The assumption that they need a partner (they romanticise life, and a solo Pujo is still a love story). Anything that frames romance as a numbers game: swipes, scores, "hot or not". Gendered clichés about who wears what or who pays.
